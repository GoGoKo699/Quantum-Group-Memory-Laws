# From commutant algebras to spatial spin memory

The single background source for this reading route is Sanjay Moudgalya and
Olexei I. Motrunich, **[Hilbert Space Fragmentation and Commutant Algebras,
arXiv:2108.10324v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10324v2)**, published in
*Physical Review X* **12**, 011050 (2022). It is a research article with
self-contained pedagogical sections. Section references below use its
[v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10324v2).

Assume ordinary quantum mechanics, tensor products, spin addition, and linear
algebra. The question to keep in mind is: **what part of a local spin operator
lies in the full conserved algebra, and how does that part depend on position?**
The source supplies the framework; this repository evaluates that question in
its specified physical representation.

## 2. Translate the notation before calculating

The paper and repository assign different meanings to the letter $\mathcal A$.
For our spin-half chain, use this dictionary:

| Source notation | Repository meaning |
|---|---|
| Bond algebra $\mathcal A$ | $\mathcal B_q$, generated by the local Hecke operators $R_j$. |
| Conserved commutant $\mathcal C$ | $\mathcal A_q$, the full represented quantum-group algebra. |
| Sector label $\lambda$ | $h/2$, the spin of the irrep. |
| $D_\lambda$ | Physical multiplicity $m_{L,h}$. |
| $d_\lambda$ | Irrep dimension $h+1$. |

The tensor factors appear in the opposite order in our convention. For finite
real $q\ge1$,

$$
\mathcal H_L=\bigoplus_h\mathcal V_{h/2}\otimes\mathbb C^{m_{L,h}},
\qquad
\mathcal A_q=\bigoplus_h\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V_{h/2})\otimes I_{m_{L,h}}.
$$

Here the allowed $h$ have the parity of $L$, and

$$
m_{L,h}=\binom L{(L-h)/2}-\binom L{(L-h)/2-1}.
$$

These are ordinary physical dimensions. Deformation changes the embeddings of
the sectors and the conserved operators, while these dimensions stay fixed.
In particular $\dim\mathcal A_q=\binom{L+3}{3}$: our representation has a
polynomial-size conserved algebra. The paper's other Temperley–Lieb
representations must not supply substitute trace weights here.

Use the [theorem's symmetric coproduct](THEOREM.md) for explicit matrices; the
source's displayed quantum-group singlet uses the reversed orientation.

## 3. Derive the quantity whose spatial law we evaluate

Use the physical inner product
$\langle O,Q\rangle=2^{-L}\operatorname{Tr}(O^\dagger Q)$.
Within sector $h$, every operator in $\mathcal A_q$ has the form
$B_h\otimes I_{m_{L,h}}$. Let $P_h$ project onto that sector. The orthogonal
projection of an arbitrary operator $O$ is therefore

$$
\Pi_q(O)=\bigoplus_h
\left[\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_{m_{L,h}}(P_hOP_h)}{m_{L,h}}
\otimes I_{m_{L,h}}\right].
$$

To check the denominator, take the inner product of the residual with any
$B_h\otimes I$. The partial trace of the first term is
$\operatorname{Tr}_{m_{L,h}}(P_hOP_h)$, while that of the proposed projection is
the same matrix. Their difference is zero. Operators between different sectors
are orthogonal to the block-diagonal conserved algebra.

For the local Pauli operator $X_i$, define
$T_h=\operatorname{Tr}_{m_{L,h}}(P_hX_iP_h)$. Since the unnormalized
Hilbert–Schmidt norm of $I_m$ has square $m$, the projection formula gives

$$
M_{L,i}(q)=2^{-L}\|\Pi_q(X_i)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=2^{-L}\sum_h\frac{\|T_h\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}{m_{L,h}}.
$$

This is the starting expression in the [theorem](THEOREM.md). The multiplicity
trace is taken **before squaring**. Our Pauli convention has
$2^{-L}\operatorname{Tr}(X_i^2)=1$; using $S_i^x=X_i/2$ instead divides the
correlation and projection weight by four.

For an isolated Hamiltonian preserving $\mathcal A_q$, its invariant operator
space contains this algebra. Projection onto that larger space gives the
finite-size time average, hence $\overline C_i\ge M_{L,i}$. The
[plateau dictionary](PLATEAU_DICTIONARY.md) identifies the additional nonnegative
term. In the specified [averaged local-noise model](LOCAL_REALIZATION.md), the
stationary space is exactly $\mathcal A_q$, so $C_i(t)\to M_{L,i}$ at fixed finite
$L$ and positive noise strength.

### A calculation to do by hand

At $q=1$, averaging $X_i$ over all site permutations projects onto the collective
spin algebra. Thus

$$
\Pi_1(X_i)=\frac1L\sum_{j=1}^L X_j,
\qquad
2^{-L}\operatorname{Tr}(X_jX_k)=\delta_{jk},
\qquad M_{L,i}(1)=\frac1L.
$$

This checks the normalization and shows why every site agrees without
diagonalizing a Hamiltonian. At $q>1$, computing the multiplicity trace in the
deformed spin basis is the next step. Projecting only onto the raising and
lowering generators omits their products; the
[explicit two-generator comparison](../literature/ATTRIBUTION.md#observable-normalization)
shows why that smaller projection gives a different answer.

## 4. Continue from the framework to the result

Read the [physical mechanism](PHYSICAL_MECHANISM.md) next. It writes the exact
answer as a sector probability times a sector-conditioned overlap:

$$
M_{L,i}=\sum_h p_{L,h}\eta_{L,i;h},\qquad
p_{L,h}=\frac{(h+1)m_{L,h}}{2^L}.
$$

The probabilities are independent of $q$, with typical $h$ of order $\sqrt L$.
The deformed end has appreciable overlap on a bounded range of magnetic-ladder
entries, giving the $L^{-1/2}$ memory law. In the proved bulk regime, many smaller
entries give $L^{-2}$. These are overlaps inside spin sectors, not a law of
transport in physical time. The uniform proof controls the sectors omitted by
this power-counting explanation.

The end law fixes finite $q>1$. The bulk law fixes $q>q_0$ and an interior
position fraction; $q_0$ is a sufficient proof threshold. Both contributions
vanish as $L$ grows. The theorem specifies the different boundary-distance and
weak-deformation limit issues. The finite-size noisy realization leaves the
system-size dependence of its relaxation gap unevaluated.

## 5. Check your understanding

Before following the detailed recursion, try explaining why the following four
statements hold. Each answer has a short route in this guide.

1. The conserved algebra includes products of generators, not just their span.
2. The projection contains $1/m_{L,h}$ because it averages over physical copies
   of an irrep.
3. Changing $q$ can change local memory without changing sector dimensions.
4. A Hamiltonian lower bound and the specified noisy-model equality are
   compatible statements about different invariant operator spaces.

The guide supplies the entry point to the existing derivation. The evaluated
spatial law and its uniform proof remain the repository's substantive next step
beyond the tutorial framework.
