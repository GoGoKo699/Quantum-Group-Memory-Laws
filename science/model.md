# The stationary physical channel and local erasure

## Physical representation

Use an open chain of $`n`$ physical spin-$`\tfrac12`$ sites with the ordinary Hilbert space $`\mathcal H_n=(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}`$ and ordinary trace. Write $`|0\rangle`$ for up, $`|1\rangle`$ for down, and fix a finite real deformation $`r\gt1`$. The symmetric coproduct is

```math
E_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\left(\prod_{j\lt i}r^{-Z_j/2}\right)s_i^+
\left(\prod_{j\gt i}r^{Z_j/2}\right),\qquad
F_n=E_n^\dagger,\qquad K_n=r^{\sum_jZ_j/2},\qquad
s_i^+=(X_i+iY_i)/2.
```

The unital star-algebra generated by all products of these operators is $`\mathcal A_r`$. The stationary channel $`\Pi_r`$ is the orthogonal projection onto this **whole algebra** in the ordinary Hilbert–Schmidt inner product. The [spatial-memory theorem](../research/THEOREM.md) writes the same deformation as $`q`$ and chain length as $`L`$; throughout the physical-erasure account they are $`r`$ and $`n`$. Erasure probability is always $`p`$.

The physical sector decomposition is

```math
\mathcal H_n=\bigoplus_{j=j_{\min}}^{n/2}V_j\otimes M_j,
\qquad d_j=2j+1,\qquad
m_j=\binom n{n/2-j}-\binom n{n/2-j-1}.
```

The decomposition is understood through its physical, orthonormal $`r`$-Clebsch–Gordan isometry $`W`$. These are ordinary dimensions and multiplicities, rather than quantum-dimension weights. Let $`P_j`$ project onto a physical sector and set

```math
X_j=\mathrm{Tr}_{M_j}(W_j^\dagger P_j X P_jW_j).
```

Then

```math
\Pi_r(X)=\bigoplus_j X_j\otimes\frac{I_{M_j}}{m_j},
\qquad \sum_j\mathrm{Tr}X_j=\mathrm{Tr}X.
```

Every sector extraction is completely positive and trace nonincreasing. Thus the formula is a channel identity after tensoring an arbitrary identity on a reference. It removes inter-sector coherences and the input multiplicity register, retains each unnormalized logical matrix $`X_j`$, and mixes the physical copies of that irrep uniformly.

For multiplicity/path embeddings $`W_{j,\alpha}:V_j\to\mathcal H_n`$, the same identity is

```math
\Pi_r(X)=\sum_j\frac1{m_j}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m_j}
W_{j,\alpha}X_jW_{j,\alpha}^\dagger.
```

At $`r=1`$ use the ordinary collective spin algebra. Its projection is exactly permutation twirling. At finite $`r\gt1`$, the represented algebra has the same block dimensions but different physical embeddings. A global algebra isomorphism does not identify subsequent tensor-factor loss maps.

## Local physical erasure

For $`0\lt p\lt1`$, define receiver-flagged erasure

```math
\mathcal E_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho\oplus
p\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)|e\rangle\langle e|,
\qquad
\mathcal N_{n,r,p}=\mathcal E_p^{\otimes n}\circ\Pi_r.
```

The receiver sees the erased positions. Loss acts on the actual physical sites after the full projection. There is no deformed inner product or simultaneous conjugation of the loss channel. A reference system is unaffected. Independent uses have fresh projection environments and fresh independent erasures; no quantum bypass or initially shared entanglement is supplied.

One use is the entire $`n`$-qubit block. The two-way quantum capacity $`Q_2(\mathcal N_{n,r,p})`$ is the asymptotic entanglement-distribution rate in qubits **per complete block**, allowing unlimited LOCC between independent calls and vanishing error as the number of calls grows at fixed $`n`$. The theorem asks whether this per-block rate can grow when $`n`$ subsequently grows. It proves a ceiling depending only on $`r,p`$.

## Projection supplied versus projection implemented

Here $`\Pi_r`$ is the supplied channel. The [local realization](../research/LOCAL_REALIZATION.md) proves its fixed-size stationary attainment by unrecorded symmetry-preserving bond kicks, with coherent dynamics commuting with the whole algebra. Its dissipative gap is size dependent and its scaling is not established. A finite-time implementation must separately control its reference-stable channel error and mixing cost. The present capacity theorem makes no claim about the rate per physical time of that implementation.
