## 1. Why this question was selected

Delacrétaz, Gorbenko, Wang, Zan and Zhabin [D26] report slowly decaying transverse-spin correlations and unusual finite-size late-time behavior in quantum-group-symmetric spin chains. Their Sec. IV compares the plateau with a single-charge Mazur bound; Appendix F explicitly improves the bound with nonlinear charges, displaying up to thirteen at L=10. They do not claim the single-charge bound is tight.

The discriminating question is: **how much finite-size memory is required by the entire symmetry algebra before one attributes additional memory to a selected Hamiltonian or its relaxation law?**

Full-commutant Mazur projections are an established framework [MM22]. This pilot's additional calculation is an efficient exact finite-size evaluation for the real-q spin-1/2 representation used in [D26]. The result may become useful for interpreting the reported plateau, but its significance is not established by a large ratio to a deliberately incomplete bound.

## 2. Fixed system and normalization

Take L spin-1/2 sites with open boundaries. Let q be finite and real, q>=1. The undeformed endpoint q=1 is ordinary SU(2). Write X,Y,Z for Pauli matrices and s^+=(X+iY)/2. Use the symmetric coproduct, so

$$
E_L=\sum_{i=1}^L
 \left(\prod_{j<i}q^{-Z_j/2}\right)s_i^+
 \left(\prod_{j>i}q^{Z_j/2}\right),\qquad
F_L=E_L^\dagger,\qquad K_L=q^{\sum_j Z_j/2}.
$$

Let \(\mathcal A_q\) be the unital *-algebra generated by E,F,K, including all products and linear combinations. At q=1, E and F also generate total Z through their commutator.

The observable is the Pauli-normalized infinite-temperature autocorrelation

$$
C_i(t)=2^{-L}\operatorname{Tr}[X_i(t)X_i],\qquad C_i(0)=1.
$$

For a closed Hamiltonian, the late-time quantity means its Cesàro average

$$
\overline C_i=\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\int_0^T C_i(t)\,dt,
$$

not a claim of pointwise convergence in a finite spectrum. All plateau numbers below use this X normalization. No numerical value was read off a source's sigma-plus plot.

## 3. Complete symmetry projection

Standard real-q representation theory gives

$$
\mathcal H_L=\bigoplus_j\left(\mathcal V_j\otimes\mathbb C^{m_{L,j}}\right),
\quad
m_{L,j}=\binom L{L/2-j}-\binom L{L/2-j-1},
\quad \dim\mathcal V_j=2j+1.
$$

Out-of-range binomial coefficients are zero; j takes the usual parity-compatible spins. The symmetry acts irreducibly on V_j and identically on the multiplicity factor. Its image is

$$
\mathcal A_q=\bigoplus_j\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V_j)\otimes I_{m_{L,j}}.
$$

Its dimension is \(\binom{L+3}{3}\), not exponential. Thus this spin-1/2 example is not being claimed to exhibit Hilbert-space fragmentation in the exponential-commutant definition of [MM22].

Let P_j select the j sector. The Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal projection onto this algebra is

$$
\Pi_q(O)=\bigoplus_j
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}_{m_{L,j}}(P_jOP_j)}{m_{L,j}}\otimes I_{m_{L,j}}.
$$

Define

$$
\boxed{
M_{L,i}(q)=2^{-L}\|\Pi_q(X_i)\|_{\rm HS}^2
 =2^{-L}\sum_j
 \frac{\|\operatorname{Tr}_{m_{L,j}}(P_jX_iP_j)\|_{\rm HS}^2}{m_{L,j}}.
}
$$

For every Hamiltonian commuting with the symmetry,

$$
\boxed{\overline C_i\ge M_{L,i}(q).}
$$

Proof: infinite-time averaging is the orthogonal projection onto the operators commuting with H. That space contains A_q. Nested orthogonal projections give the inequality. The projection/Mazur principle and its full-algebra use are inherited [MM22], not a new conservation theorem.

## 4. Exact recurrence without constructing the full Hilbert space

A q-Clebsch--Gordan basis couples one site at a time. A multiplicity path records the intermediate total spins. For a site i already present, define

$$
T_{n,i}(J,M)=
 \sum_{r=1}^{m_{n,J}}
 \langle J,M+1;r|s_i^+|J,M;r\rangle.
$$

Only these charge-raising matrix elements are needed:

$$
\boxed{
M_{L,i}(q)=\frac{2}{2^L}
 \sum_J\frac1{m_{L,J}}\sum_{M=-J}^{J-1}|T_{L,i}(J,M)|^2.
}
$$

The factor two includes the orthogonal lowering matrix elements of X. This is a partial trace over all multiplicity paths, not a selected subset of charges.

### Clebsch--Gordan coefficients

For parent spin j and total magnetic quantum number M, put D=2j+1 and n=j+M+1/2. Define

$$
a_j(M)=\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{-2n}}{1-q^{-2D}}},
\qquad
b_j(M)=q^{-n}\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{-2(D-n)}}{1-q^{-2D}}}.
$$

For J=j+1/2, the coefficients of
\(|j,M-1/2\rangle|\uparrow\rangle\) and
\(|j,M+1/2\rangle|\downarrow\rangle\)
are a and b. For J=j-1/2 they are -b and a.
Invalid magnetic states have coefficient zero.

At q=1 take the continuous limits \(a=\sqrt{n/D}\) and
\(b=\sqrt{(D-n)/D}\). The checker independently builds the complete coupled basis at small L, verifies orthonormality, and verifies that the resulting projection commutes with each local Hecke generator.

Denote these coefficients by C(j,J,M,s), with s=+/-1/2. The multiplicities satisfy
\(m_{n,J}=m_{n-1,J-1/2}+m_{n-1,J+1/2}\).
At the insertion site,

$$
T_{i,i}(J,M)=\sum_{j=J\pm1/2}m_{i-1,j}
 C(j,J,M+1,+1/2)C(j,J,M,-1/2).
$$

For n>i,

$$
\boxed{
T_{n,i}(J,M)=\sum_{j=J\pm1/2}\sum_{s=\pm1/2}
 C(j,J,M+1,s)C(j,J,M,s)T_{n-1,i}(j,M-s).
}
$$

This follows by expanding both states at the last coupled spin and tracing identical multiplicity paths. No time evolution or approximation enters.

At each layer there are O(n^2) entries and constant work per entry. Thus for one specified site the recurrence requires O(L^3) arithmetic operations and O(L^2) working entries. This is arithmetic complexity, not a numerical bit-complexity or hardware runtime theorem. The implementation propagates \(2^{-n}m\) and \(2^{-n}T\) to avoid exponentially large multiplicities. Double precision is validated over the reported sizes; arbitrary-L accuracy is not proved.

## 5. One charge can drastically undercount symmetry memory

Projection only onto span{E,F}, in our normalization, gives

$$
M_{\rm one}(L,q)=\frac1L
 \left[\frac{q+q^{-1}+2}{2(q+q^{-1})}\right]^{L-1}.
$$

The direct Hilbert-space calculation checks this convention. It is the single-generator comparator, not the source's best thirteen-charge construction.

At q=2.6, for the left central site i=L/2 of an even chain:

| L | One-generator bound | Complete symmetry contribution | Ratio | L^2 times complete |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 0.0197432917 | 0.0447412331 | 2.26615 | 4.47412331 |
| 20 | 0.0016275053 | 0.0142698351 | 8.76792 | 5.70793403 |
| 40 | 2.21186241e-05 | 0.00428106001 | 193.55 | 6.84969601 |
| 80 | 8.17070437e-09 | 0.0012104601 | 148146 | 7.74694464 |
| 160 | 2.22993534e-15 | 0.000327223237 | 1.46741e+11 | 8.37691486 |

These are finite-size evaluations of an exact recursion, not numerical interval certificates. The last column is retained for future scaling analysis. **It does not prove an L^-2 law.** No finite-size fit is substituted for a theorem about L tending to infinity.

At q=1, the calculation recovers exactly \(M_{L,i}=1/L\). There is a short structural proof: the undeformed symmetry algebra is permutation invariant, so all projected X_i coincide; their sum is the already conserved total X. Thus \(\Pi_1(X_i)=\sum_jX_j/L\).

The source already emphasizes that nonlinear conserved charges improve the q-deformed bound [D26, App. F]. The present result completes the specified algebraic projection and evaluates it at much larger sizes. It is not discovery of the usefulness of additional conserved quantities.

## 6. A local noise model that attains this contribution exactly

A mathematical tightness control uses unrecorded random local unitary kicks, not the source's deterministic closed Hamiltonian.

In the basis up-up, up-down, down-up, down-down, use the inherited Hecke matrix

$$
R(q)=
\begin{pmatrix}
q&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&1&q-q^{-1}&0\\
0&0&0&q
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its eigenvalues are q and -q^-1. Let \(P_j=(qI-R_j)/(q+q^{-1})\) and
\(U_j=I-2P_j\). Each U_j is a Hermitian two-site unitary that commutes with the global quantum-group generators. Define

$$
\mathcal L^\dagger(O)=\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\gamma_j(U_jOU_j-O),\qquad\gamma_j>0.
$$

This describes Poisson-distributed local unitary kicks averaged without conditioning on their times. It changes the dynamical model; it is not an implicit approximation to coherent Hamiltonian evolution.

With the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt inner product,

$$
-\langle O,\mathcal L^\dagger(O)\rangle
 =\frac12\sum_j\gamma_j\|[U_j,O]\|_2^2.
$$

The zero eigenspace is the commutant of all local R_j. Standard q-Schur--Weyl duality identifies it with A_q for the finite real-q spin-1/2 representation. Since the generator is self-adjoint and nonpositive, its long-time limit is exactly Pi_q. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}C_i^{\rm random\ kicks}(t)=M_{L,i}(q).}
$$

This is a standard commutant-preserving dissipation argument, not a new general steady-state principle; [LSP23] explicitly relates structure-preserving dissipation and full-commutant memory. The current application proves tightness in this declared noisy model. No relaxation gap, transport exponent, noise robustness, or protected logical-qubit fidelity is inferred.

The four-site superoperator has dimension 256 and exactly 35 stationary operators; its stationary projection matches the recurrence at every site. The normalized plateau vector at q=2.6 is
(0.2710324357, 0.1881338669, 0.1881338669, 0.2710324357).

## 7. Closed-Hamiltonian control: the full symmetry contribution is not the full plateau

The checker also implements the source's open XXZ Hamiltonian with the quantum-group-preserving three-site deformation [D26, Sec. II]:

$$
H=H_0+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{L-2}V_j,\qquad
\Delta=(q+q^{-1})/2,
$$
$$
H_0=\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}(X_jX_{j+1}+Y_jY_{j+1}+\Delta Z_jZ_{j+1})
-\frac{q-q^{-1}}2(Z_1-Z_L).
$$

Here V_j is implemented literally from the source's Eqs. (5)-(7), with sums restricted to sites inside the open chain. The full expression is in `source_hamiltonian` in the checker. Relative commutator errors with E are below 4e-15 in the completed tests.

For L=8, q=2.6, i=4,

$$
M_{8,4}=0.06401021524,
$$

while exact finite-spectrum time averages give

$$
\overline C_{4}=0.08417515096\quad(\lambda=0),\qquad
\overline C_{4}=0.08608131877\quad(\lambda=1).
$$

The calculation uses all degenerate spectral blocks and agrees when the degeneracy tolerance changes from 1e-9 to 1e-10. These are small-system numerical checks, not certified spectral intervals or evidence of chaos.

A selected Hamiltonian has additional invariant operators, including spectral projectors. Algebraic memory need not exhaust its time average. It would be incorrect to declare that the complete projection explains every observed plateau, or to subtract a hypothesized thermodynamic power law and infer a dynamical exponent from these eight-spin examples.

The exact projection formula is inherited from the full-commutant Mazur framework [MM22]. The q-deformed spin-chain algebra, its local Hecke generators and the expectation that nonlinear charges matter are inherited as well [D26; MM22]. The saturation mechanism under structure-preserving noise has a direct framework predecessor [LSP23].

The additional calculation here is the q-Clebsch--Gordan multiplicity-trace recursion for the entire transverse-spin symmetry contribution, with a computationally explicit finite-size answer and a clean separation from a fixed-H plateau. No inspected passage supplies this particular recurrence and resulting size dependence. This is a bounded source comparison, not exhaustive novelty clearance. Standard representation-theoretic computation may make the recursion itself a technical contribution rather than a major physical advance.

The independently consequential unresolved question is the finite-size law and spatial distribution of unavoidable transverse memory, and how it constrains the interpretation of the reported late-time behavior. A next pass should derive controlled large-L behavior of M, rather than infer an exponent from the table. Only after that should it test how much of a selected Hamiltonian's plateau remains unexplained. This does not require a full theory of superdiffusion before the symmetry question is worth answering.

**One bounded GO; no repository or PRL commitment.** The calculation is a way to separate two explanations, not a completed explanation of anomalous hydrodynamics. If further work yields only another equivalent way to calculate a known projection, reassess rather than extending automatically.

## 9. Second candidate: Gaussian logical phase gates

The independent operation question was whether oscillator phase rotations can implement non-Clifford logical gates while retaining useful photon-loss protection. [W26] already constructs precisely that tradeoff, including cyclic cat and Vandermonde codes and explicit error-detection procedures. The generic formulation therefore does not advance to a redundant calculation. This rejects neither bosonic codes as a field nor separately motivated operations. No new gap beyond the inspected construction was isolated.
