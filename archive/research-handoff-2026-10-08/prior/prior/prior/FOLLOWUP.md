## 1. Fixed question and claim hierarchy

Retain the open L-site spin-1/2 representation with the symmetric real-q coproduct, q>=1, and the infinite-temperature Pauli normalization

$$
C_i(t)=2^{-L}\operatorname{Tr}[X_i(t)X_i],\qquad C_i(0)=1.
$$

The complete symmetry contribution is

$$
M_{L,i}(q)=2^{-L}\|\Pi_q(X_i)\|_{\rm HS}^2,
$$

where Pi_q projects onto the full unital *-algebra generated by the global quantum-group generators. For any symmetry-preserving Hamiltonian,

$$
\overline C_i\ge M_{L,i}(q).
$$

This is a finite-system infinite-time average; it does not identify an intermediate-time transport exponent. The full-algebra projection principle is inherited [MM22]. The source [D26] already emphasizes that nonlinear charges improve its single-generator bound. Neither fact receives a new-priority claim here.

**New claims established in this pass:**

1. For fixed finite q>1,
   $$M_{L,1}(q)=M_{L,L}(q)=\frac{\tanh(\log q)}{\sqrt{2\pi L}}+O_q(L^{-1}).$$
2. For each fixed nonnegative distance d from either end, the exact leading coefficient of the same L^-1/2 law is given by a tridiagonal operator below.
3. For every fixed q>sqrt(3) and epsilon in (0,1/2), there are positive c(q,epsilon), L_0(q,epsilon) such that
   $$M_{L,i}(q)\ge c(q,\epsilon)L^{-2}$$
   for L>=L_0 and epsilon L<=i<=(1-epsilon)L. This is a lower bound, not an equality or a physical transition at q=sqrt(3).
4. In the auxiliary ordered limit q->infinity at fixed L, followed by L->infinity, the entire bulk scaling function can be evaluated exactly. This limit is not interchanged with the fixed-finite-q large-L problem.

At q=1 the preceding exact answer M=1/L remains unchanged. The edge expansion is not uniform in q approaching one.

## 2. Doubled-spin notation

Let h=2j be the nonnegative integer total-spin label and set

$$
m_{n,h}=\binom n{(n-h)/2}-\binom n{(n-h)/2-1},\qquad
D_n(h)=2^{-n}m_{n,h}.
$$

The value is zero outside 0<=h<=n with matching parity. A useful equivalent identity is

$$m_{n,h}=\frac{h+1}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{(n-h)/2}.$$

Write r=M+j in the raising matrix element, so 0<=r<h. Let t_{n,i}(h,r)=2^{-n}T_{n,i}(j,M), using the previous multiplicity trace T. Then

$$M_{L,i}=2\sum_{h\ge1}\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}\frac{t_{L,i}(h,r)^2}{D_L(h)}.$$

Put z=q^-2, v_r=1-z^(r+1). The new grid implementation is a vectorization of the unchanged Clebsch-Gordan recurrence, not a different physical model.

## 3. Exact boundary sum

At the last site i=L, the insertion formula reduces to

$$
T_{L,L}(h,r)=q^{-r}\sqrt{(1-z^{r+1})(1-z^{h-r})}
\left[\frac{m_{L-1,h-1}}{1-z^h}
-z\frac{m_{L-1,h+1}}{1-z^{h+2}}\right].
$$

Define the multiplicity fractions

$$
p^-_{L,h}=\frac{h(L+h+2)}{2L(h+1)},\qquad
p^+_{L,h}=\frac{(h+2)(L-h)}{2L(h+1)},\qquad p^-+p^+=1.
$$

The magnetic-index sum is elementary:

$$
S_h(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}z^r(1-z^{r+1})(1-z^{h-r})
=\frac{(1-z^h)(1+z^{h+2})}{1-z^2}-hz^h.
$$

Therefore the complete edge memory, not a selected-generator bound, is exactly

$$
\boxed{
M_{L,L}(q)=2\sum_{\substack{h\ge1\\h\equiv L\ (2)}}D_L(h)
\left[\frac{p^-_{L,h}}{1-z^h}-z\frac{p^+_{L,h}}{1-z^{h+2}}\right]^2S_h(z).
}\tag{1}
$$

Reflection followed by a global spin flip preserves the symmetry algebra and maps X_i to X_{L+1-i}. This gives the equality of the two ends and the reflection symmetry of M.

### Asymptotic proof, without fitting

For fixed q>1,

$$
\frac{p^-_{L,h}}{1-z^h}-z\frac{p^+_{L,h}}{1-z^{h+2}}
=\frac{1-z}{2}+O_q\left(\frac1{h+1}+\frac{h+1}{L}+z^h\right),
$$

and S_h=(1-z^2)^-1+O_q((h+1)z^h). Uniform constants exist for all h>=1. All factors in (1) stay bounded at fixed q.

The exact multiplicity sums imply

$$
\sum_hD_L(h)=2^{-L}\binom L{\lfloor L/2\rfloor},\quad
\sum_h(h+1)D_L(h)=1,\quad
\sum_h\frac{D_L(h)}{h+1}=O(L^{-1}).
$$

Also D_L(h)<=C(h+1)L^-3/2, so the exponentially weighted errors sum to O_q(L^-3/2). Inserting these estimates into (1) yields

$$
M_{L,L}=\frac{1-z}{2(1+z)}2^{-L}\binom L{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}+O_q(L^{-1}).
$$

The h=0 term omitted from (1) changes this intermediate expression by only O(L^-3/2). Stirling's formula now proves

$$
\boxed{M_{L,1}=\frac{1-z}{1+z}\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi L}}+O_q(L^{-1}).}\tag{2}
$$

The coefficient is tanh(log q), not a fitted normalization. For q=2.6 it is 0.2961221050402387 in sqrt(L) M.

The boundary law vanishes as L grows. It is not a finite thermodynamic edge plateau or a statement that a specific Hamiltonian has no additional zero modes. The already constructed local random-kick semigroup has exactly this complete symmetry value at late times, so it is achievable as a late-time law in that declared noisy model. It says nothing about its relaxation times.

## 4. Every fixed distance from the end

For a fixed d>=0, observe i=L-d. As L tends to infinity, all but a negligible fraction of the multiplicity sum lies at h growing as sqrt(L). In the finitely many d extension steps, denominators involving z^h tend to one, while changes in D_L(h) under fixed shifts are negligible at leading order.

Define an operator J_q on nonnegative integer r by

$$
(J_q)_{r,r+1}=\tfrac12\sqrt{(1-z^{r+1})(1-z^{r+2})},
\qquad
(J_q)_{r,r}=\tfrac12(q^{-1}+q^{-3})z^r,
$$

with symmetric off-diagonals and no negative-index entry. Let

$$f_r=q^{-r}\sqrt{1-z^{r+1}}.$$

The insertion profile divided by the large-h multiplicity tends to (1-z)f/2; each subsequent step applies J_q. A dominated sum is valid because f has an exponential tail and d is fixed. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}\sqrt L\,M_{L,L-d}(q)
=\frac{(1-z)^2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\|J_q^d f\|_{\ell^2}^2.
}\tag{3}
$$

This also applies at i=d+1 by reflection. At d=0, ||f||^2=(1-z^2)^-1, recovering (2).

For q=2.6, the coefficients at d=0,1,2,3,4 are approximately
0.2961221050, 0.1316798307, 0.0842719340, 0.0608044292, 0.0468316484.
They describe sites held a fixed distance from an end. No uniform formula for d proportional to L follows by substituting d into this fixed-d limit.

## 5. Finite-q bulk memory has a polynomial lower bound

This proof establishes a genuine finite-q statement. It does not take q to infinity.

### Positivity in a declared parameter regime

The full insertion at site i is

$$
t_{i,i}(h,r)=2^{-i}q^{-r}\sqrt{(1-z^{r+1})(1-z^{h-r})}
\left[\frac{m_{i-1,h-1}}{1-z^h}-z\frac{m_{i-1,h+1}}{1-z^{h+2}}\right].
$$

Where m_{i-1,h-1}>0,

$$
\frac{m_{i-1,h+1}}{m_{i-1,h-1}}
=\frac{h+2}{h}\frac{i-h}{i+h+2}\le3.
$$

Hence every insertion entry is nonnegative when z<1/3. In particular,

$$t_{i,i}(h,0)\ge 2^{-i}c_0(q)m_{i-1,h-1},\qquad
c_0(q)=(1-3z)\sqrt{1-z}>0.$$

All subsequent recurrence coefficients are nonnegative: they are products of Clebsch-Gordan coefficients with the same branch and local spin. It is therefore legitimate to discard positive paths when proving a lower bound. This positivity shortcut is the reason for q>sqrt(3). Nothing singular in the finite system is asserted at that value.

### A surviving set of paths

Keep only steps that change (h,r) together by (+1,+1) or (-1,-1). They preserve u=h-r>=1. Their coefficients are at least

$$\tfrac12\sqrt{v_rv_{r+1}}$$

on the r-to-r+1 edge, since the discarded h-dependent denominators are at most one. Let J_0 be this tridiagonal matrix with zero diagonal, and set w_n(r)=(J_0^n)_{r,0}.

For p=i-1, n=L-i and a>=0 of parity p,

$$
\boxed{
t_{L,i}(a+1+r,r)\ge c_0(q)\frac{m_{p,a}}{2^i}w_n(r).
}\tag{4}
$$

This is a term-by-term lower bound in the complete quantum-algebra recursion. The paths are a counting device, not a replacement of the quantum system by a stochastic spin model.

### Weighted ballot paths retain a nonzero fraction

Let K_n(x,y) be the probability kernel of a simple symmetric walk on nonnegative integers, killed upon hitting -1. Reflection gives

$$
K_n(x,y)=2^{-n}\left[\binom n{(n+y-x)/2}-\binom n{(n+y+x+2)/2}\right].
$$

All out-of-range and wrong-parity binomials vanish. In particular K_n(0,r)=2^-n m_{n,r}.

For r in [sqrt(n),2sqrt(n)] of the correct parity, there exists c_1(q)>0, independent of n and r, such that

$$w_n(r)\ge c_1(q)K_n(0,r).\tag{5}$$

Here is a uniform proof. On a nonnegative bridge from 0 to r, the weight relative to the simple walk is

$$\sqrt{v_0v_r}\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}v_{R_t}.$$

Let N_s count its interior visits to s. The following estimates follow from the sine representation, reflection formula, and Stirling's formula:

$$K_m(x,y)\le C\frac{(x+1)(y+1)}{(m+1)^{3/2}},\qquad
K_n(0,r)\ge c\frac{r+1}{n^{3/2}}$$

in the stated endpoint window and parity. For the first estimate use

$$K_m(x,y)=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\cos^m\theta\sin[(x+1)\theta]\sin[(y+1)\theta]d\theta,$$

bound each sine by its index times distance from {0,pi}, and integrate a Gaussian bound on |cos(theta)|^m. The Green function is exactly

$$\sum_{m\ge0}K_m(x,y)=2\min(x+1,y+1).$$

Splitting the bridge at n/2 and applying these two estimates gives

$$\mathbb E[N_s\mid R_n=r,\ R_t\ge0]\le C'(s+1)$$

uniformly in n,r. Jensen's inequality consequently implies

$$
\frac{w_n(r)}{K_n(0,r)}
\ge(1-z)\exp\left[-C'\sum_{s\ge0}(s+1)|\log(1-z^{s+1})|\right]>0.
$$

The series is finite at every fixed q>1. This proves (5); the proof does not use a numerically fitted visit-count bound.

### Sum over typical spin sectors

Choose a in [sqrt(p),2sqrt(p)] and r in [sqrt(n),2sqrt(n)] with their allowed parities. For interior sites, p,n are both proportional to L. Uniform Stirling estimates give

$$m_{p,a}=\Theta(2^p/L),\quad m_{n,r}=\Theta(2^n/L),\quad
m_{L,a+r+1}=\Theta(2^L/L).$$

Each (a,r) contribution to the memory lower bound is at least a constant times L^-3, by (4)-(5). There are order L such pairs. Therefore

$$
\boxed{M_{L,i}(q)\ge c(q,\epsilon)L^{-2}.}\tag{6}
$$

Constants may deteriorate near q=sqrt(3) or the spatial boundaries. No optimal constant is claimed. This excludes exponentially small *complete* symmetry memory in the stated regime. It does not prove that L^-2 is the exact finite-q bulk decay or rule out a larger polynomial floor.

## 6. An exactly soluble auxiliary limit

The q-Clebsch-Gordan coefficients have a pointwise q->infinity limit at each fixed L. Denote the resulting limit of the projection value by M^cr. This is not the full commutant of a separately rescaled q=infinity Hamiltonian; that singular Hamiltonian can have additional invariants.

In this limit only the paths retained in Section 5 survive, and the insertion is at r=0. Thus, with p=i-1, n=L-i,

$$
\boxed{M^{\rm cr}_{L,i}=2^{1-L}\sum_{a,b}
\frac{m_{p,a}^2m_{n,b}^2}{m_{L,a+b+1}}.}\tag{7}
$$

The ranges and parity are those of their multiplicities. At i/L->x in (0,1), uniform Stirling asymptotics followed by a dominated Riemann sum yields

$$M^{\rm cr}_{L,i}=L^{-2}\mathcal F(x)[1+o(1)],$$

where

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal F(x)=\frac{3\sqrt2}{4\pi[x(1-x)]^3}
\int_0^1\frac{u^2(1-u)^2\,du}
{\left[\frac12+\frac{(u-x)^2}{x(1-x)}\right]^{5/2}}.
}\tag{8}
$$

A derivation uses a=A sqrt(L), b=B sqrt(L). The spin multiplicity asymptotic is

$$2^{-n}m_{n,h}\sim2\sqrt{2/\pi}(h+1)n^{-3/2}e^{-(h+1)^2/(2n)}.$$

The parity lattice has area 4/L. In the continuum integral change variables A+B=s, A/(A+B)=u, then integrate s^4 exp(-Ds^2), giving 3 sqrt(pi)/(8D^(5/2)). Gaussian bounds away from the scaling window control the tails; small a or b give vanishing endpoint contributions. This gives (8), including its normalization.

For example F(1/2)=2.6505930176794874 and F(1/4)=3.809143396177682.
**Neither is the coefficient of the fixed-q=2.6 bulk law.** Equation (7) is an exact control and an explanation of the ballot-path mechanism. It is not permission to exchange q and L limits.

## 8. Physical interpretation and source comparison

At every fixed q>1, the exact symmetry-enforced edge memory is parametrically larger than the SU(2) contribution 1/L. It still tends to zero. This is therefore enhanced finite-size memory, not a protected edge qubit or a nonzero thermodynamic edge memory.

In the finite-q regime of (6), the full bulk symmetry contribution is at least algebraic even though the single-generator bound decays exponentially. That is a substantive restriction on extrapolations based on one charge. It does not contradict [D26]: the authors already state that their one-charge estimate is not tight and explicitly include nonlinear charges in Appendix F.

[MM22] supplies the complete-algebra projection and explains spin-1/2 quantum-group multiplets in its Temperley-Lieb discussion. Its spin-m TL ensembles have different physical multiplicity weights from the spin-1/2 representation used here. Matching an abstract bond algebra alone does not equate infinite-temperature local autocorrelations.

[H24] derives exact boundary and bulk memory from conserved-pattern counting in pair-/p-flip models. It already establishes why finite-size fits can misidentify memory exponents and why boundary and bulk must be distinguished. Its nonzero limiting boundary memory and L^-1/2 bulk law are not the formulas derived here. Its discussion also identifies the larger TL commutant as a separate question. We are not claiming the general discovery that nonlocal conserved quantities change boundary memory.

The additional results to assess are the explicit finite-q spin-1/2 transverse edge law and the finite-q bulk algebraic floor, following the complete non-Abelian symmetry projection. The targeted reading did not identify these formulas in the inspected sources. This is not exhaustive priority clearance, external review, or a claim that standard representation theory cannot imply them by another route.

The next useful test is a matching finite-q bulk asymptotic and its contribution-level comparison. Do not infer the reported dynamical exponent, enlarge the code into a large Hamiltonian simulation, or claim that this symmetry value exhausts every deterministic-H plateau. The prior small-H counterexamples remain unchanged.
