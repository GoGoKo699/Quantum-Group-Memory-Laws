# Thirty milestones: from a local spin to the physical-loss ceiling

This is a common map of the project's understanding targets. It uses the single educational anchor, [Watrous 2018](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md). The **12 background/preliminaries** milestones build from one physical register to the shared algebra-and-channel framework. The **18 narrative/innovation** milestones connect the spatial-memory calculation and the physical-erasure argument, then integrate their distinct meanings. Specialist proof details remain in the linked scientific accounts.

## Background and preliminaries

### M01 — Physical sites and reduced states

**Central question:** What does it mean to lose one site of a spin chain?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish the tensor factors representing physical sites from a change of basis on the whole chain.

**Dependencies and story role:** None; starts with the local carrier before any symmetry description.

**Conceptual connection:** A partial trace acts on a specified physical factor, which remains important after deformation.

**Sources:** Watrous §§2.1.1–2.1.2; [model](../science/model.md).

### M02 — References and quantum coherence

**Central question:** What must be retained to preserve information entangled with another system?

**Understanding target:** Explain why an untouched reference tests coherent transmission more strongly than a collection of unreferenced expectation values.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01; adds the external system needed to formulate the operational task.

**Conceptual connection:** The same physical channel must act correctly when tensored with an identity map.

**Sources:** Watrous §2.1.3 and §2.2.1; [model](../science/model.md).

### M03 — Complete channel descriptions

**Central question:** How does a Kraus description specify loss on every input operator?

**Understanding target:** Relate Kraus operators, trace preservation, off-diagonal inputs, and complete positivity.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M02; supplies the language for exact conditional maps.

**Conceptual connection:** A calculation on one density matrix is replaced by a reference-stable channel identity.

**Sources:** Watrous §2.2.2; [small sectors](../science/small_sectors.md).

### M04 — A full operator algebra

**Central question:** Why does conserving generators also retain their products?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish an algebra generated by operators from their linear span and identify the physical commutant relation.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M03; identifies precisely which information the stationary channel retains.

**Conceptual connection:** The projection target is the complete represented symmetry algebra.

**Sources:** Watrous §4.2.2; [model and claims](../research/MODEL_AND_CLAIMS.md), [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md).

### M05 — Ordinary permutation averaging

**Central question:** What does an unrecorded permutation remove?

**Understanding target:** Explain a physical twirl and distinguish permutation-invariant operators from inputs promised to be symmetric vectors.

**Dependencies and story role:** M03–M04; establishes the undeformed endpoint.

**Conceptual connection:** At ordinary symmetry the same invariant algebra can be reached by a physical permutation channel.

**Sources:** Watrous §§7.1.1–7.1.2; [interpretation](../science/interpretation.md).

### M06 — Irreducible information and multiplicity

**Central question:** Which tensor factor survives the full symmetry projection?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish the representation register, multiplicity register, sector label, and physical site labels.

**Dependencies and story role:** M04–M05; turns invariance into a usable block decomposition.

**Conceptual connection:** Tracing multiplicity and replacing it by a maximally mixed state defines the projection channel.

**Sources:** Watrous §7.1.2 and §2.2.2; [bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md), [model](../science/model.md).

### M07 — Entropy and an inference error

**Central question:** How can estimating a label control its residual uncertainty?

**Understanding target:** Explain posterior entropy and why a uniformly bounded integer estimation error can bound it independently of alphabet size.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M03; introduces the information quantity used in the loss converse.

**Conceptual connection:** Local observations can constrain a large logical register without requiring exact label recovery.

**Sources:** Watrous §§5.1–5.2; [first mismatch](../science/first_mismatch.md).

### M08 — Local operations and classical assistance

**Central question:** What assistance is available between communication attempts?

**Understanding target:** Separate local ancillas, classical messages, preshared entanglement, and a quantum bypass.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02–M03; fixes the allowed operational resources.

**Conceptual connection:** An upper bound must survive adaptive LOCC without granting an uncharged quantum channel.

**Sources:** Watrous §§6.1.1–6.1.2 and §6.2.2; [model](../science/model.md).

### M09 — A capacity per complete block

**Central question:** What grows in a communication rate when the chain size changes?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish physical chain size, independent block uses, logical qubits per block, and qubits per physical site.

**Dependencies and story role:** M07–M08; defines the limiting task before evaluating it.

**Conceptual connection:** The capacity is defined at fixed chain size before comparing different sizes.

**Sources:** Watrous §§8.2.1–8.2.2; [model](../science/model.md).

### M10 — A local operator's projection weight

**Central question:** What does a squared Hilbert–Schmidt overlap measure?

**Understanding target:** Read the ordinary normalized trace, the local Pauli normalization, and the distinction between an operator overlap and an entanglement measure.

**Dependencies and story role:** M04, M06; introduces the spatial-memory observable.

**Conceptual connection:** An orthogonal projection can control an autocorrelation without specifying a communication rate.

**Sources:** Watrous §1.1 and §4.2.2; [physical mechanism](../research/PHYSICAL_MECHANISM.md).

### M11 — Stationary structure and relaxation

**Central question:** Does knowing the stationary channel determine how fast it is reached?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish a fixed-point algebra, a finite-size dissipative gap, and a bound uniform in chain size.

**Dependencies and story role:** M03–M04, M10; separates the supplied channel from a dynamical implementation.

**Conceptual connection:** The stationary projection can be studied operationally while its implementation time remains separately charged.

**Sources:** Watrous §4.2.2; [local realization](../research/LOCAL_REALIZATION.md).

### M12 — The common framework

**Central question:** How can one projection support two different physical questions?

**Understanding target:** Connect the physical tensor product, sector channel, local operator overlap, erasure map, reference, and capacity definition without conflating them.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M11; integrates the preliminaries into the project's model.

**Conceptual connection:** Shared algebraic structure supplies the starting point; the observable and operational task determine the proof needed.

**Sources:** [Watrous bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md), [model and claims](../research/MODEL_AND_CLAIMS.md), [loss model](../science/model.md).

## Narrative and innovation

### M13 — A finite real deformation in physical coordinates

**Central question:** What changes when the symmetry coproduct is deformed?

**Understanding target:** Explain the nonlocal generator strings and why unchanged sector dimensions do not fix their physical embedding.

**Dependencies and story role:** M06, M12; introduces the shared mechanism beyond the ordinary endpoint.

**Conceptual connection:** The physical position dependence and the physical loss problem both enter through the deformed embedding.

**Sources:** [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md), [small sectors](../science/small_sectors.md).

### M14 — The ordinary-trace multiplicity projection

**Central question:** Why is multiplicity averaged before a projected norm is squared?

**Understanding target:** Derive the factor inverse to the physical multiplicity and reject substitution of quantum dimensions.

**Dependencies and story role:** M06, M10, M13; fixes the exact finite-size starting expression.

**Conceptual connection:** The same trace-preserving projection is both an operator map and a physical state channel.

**Sources:** [bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md), [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md), [model](../science/model.md).

### M15 — Local memory as a measured contribution

**Central question:** Which part of a local transverse polarization is forced by symmetry?

**Understanding target:** Relate a projected Pauli operator to its normalized retained-polarization readout and check the ordinary value inverse to chain length.

**Dependencies and story role:** M10, M14; connects the projection formula to a concrete observable.

**Conceptual connection:** The result measures a specified local overlap, rather than all information present in the algebra.

**Sources:** [physical mechanism](../research/PHYSICAL_MECHANISM.md), [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md).

### M16 — Summing all Clebsch–Gordan copies

**Central question:** How is the full finite-chain overlap evaluated without an exponentially large operator basis?

**Understanding target:** Explain the role of the magnetic ladder, multiplicity paths, and the trace recursion before squaring.

**Dependencies and story role:** M13–M15; identifies the exact computational object underlying the spatial laws.

**Conceptual connection:** Representation structure turns the full-algebra projection into a controlled finite-size recurrence.

**Sources:** [proof map](../research/PROOF_MAP.md), [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md).

### M17 — The edge law

**Central question:** Why can an end spin have a different power of chain length from ordinary symmetry?

**Understanding target:** Explain the sector-weight and magnetic-ladder mechanism behind the fixed finite-deformation inverse-square-root edge law.

**Dependencies and story role:** M15–M16; obtains the first spatial consequence with its order of limits.

**Conceptual connection:** The number of algebra directions stays fixed while their overlap with one end operator changes.

**Sources:** [physical mechanism](../research/PHYSICAL_MECHANISM.md), [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md).

### M18 — The proved bulk law

**Central question:** Which hypotheses permit the inverse-square bulk asymptotic?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish a fixed interior fraction, a fixed deformation, the sufficient proof threshold, and the normalized spatial profile.

**Dependencies and story role:** M16–M17; extends local information into a spatial comparison without removing proof conditions.

**Conceptual connection:** A uniform asymptotic estimate, rather than finite-size power counting, supplies the bulk equality.

**Sources:** [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md), [proof map](../research/PROOF_MAP.md).

### M19 — Symmetry memory and Hamiltonian excess

**Central question:** When is the symmetry contribution only part of a late-time average?

**Understanding target:** Separate the full symmetry algebra from the larger invariant space of a chosen Hamiltonian and explain the nonnegative excess.

**Dependencies and story role:** M11, M15–M18; places the spatial result in its dynamical setting.

**Conceptual connection:** A complete symmetry projection need not be the complete Hamiltonian projection.

**Sources:** [plateau dictionary](../research/PLATEAU_DICTIONARY.md), [model and claims](../research/MODEL_AND_CLAIMS.md).

### M20 — Attaining the stationary projection

**Central question:** What local averaged noise model removes the excess while retaining the full algebra?

**Understanding target:** Explain the Hermitian bond-reflection Dirichlet form, unrecorded kicks, and finite-size convergence statement.

**Dependencies and story role:** M11, M13–M14, M19; integrates the memory result with its declared local realization.

**Conceptual connection:** Stationary attainment supplies the channel used in the loss task, with a separate mixing-time account.

**Sources:** [local realization](../research/LOCAL_REALIZATION.md), [interpretation](../science/interpretation.md).

### M21 — Exact two-site conditional loss

**Central question:** How does finite deformation change the channel obtained by erasing the left or right spin?

**Understanding target:** Use the deformed triplet and its Kraus maps to distinguish conditional physical channels, then explain the probability-weighted achievable two-site rate that exceeds the ordinary-permutation benchmark.

**Dependencies and story role:** M03, M13–M14; opens the operational argument with a complete small example.

**Conceptual connection:** Different physical loss maps can change a finite-block communication rate without removing a size-independent ceiling.

**Sources:** [bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md), [small sectors](../science/small_sectors.md).

### M22 — The first multiplicity test

**Dependencies and story role:** M02–M03, M06, M21; tests the passage from a toy irrep to the supplied full projection.

**Sources:** [small sectors](../science/small_sectors.md), [model](../science/model.md).

### M23 — The exact backwards path process

**Sources:** [first mismatch](../science/first_mismatch.md).

### M26 — Censoring and posterior entropy

**Central question:** Why does the case with no observed mismatch not invalidate the converse?

**Understanding target:** Bound that censored event, combine both estimator tails, and infer a finite prior-independent conditional-entropy ceiling.

**Dependencies and story role:** M07, M24–M25; completes the classical information estimate.

**Conceptual connection:** Uniform estimation error controls residual uncertainty even when the logical alphabet grows.

**Sources:** Watrous §§5.1–5.2; [first mismatch](../science/first_mismatch.md).

### M27 — Returning to arbitrary quantum inputs

**Central question:** Why does a classical label estimate constrain an input coherent in that label?

**Sources:** Watrous §§2.1.3, 2.2.2, 5.1; [capacity](../science/capacity.md).

### M28 — The all-sector adaptive ceiling

**Sources:** Watrous §§6.1–6.2 and §8.2; [capacity](../science/capacity.md), [model](../science/model.md).

### M29 — What transfers from ordinary symmetric loss

**Sources:** [interpretation](../science/interpretation.md), [capacity](../science/capacity.md), [Quantum Permutation Noise Limits](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Permutation-Noise-Limits).

### M30 — The complete physical account

**Central question:** What survives, what is bounded, and what remains separately charged?

**Understanding target:** Connect the positive vacuum–one-up code and the ceiling to an order-one capacity per block; state the spatial laws with their own hypotheses and distinguish exact small maps, asymptotic estimates, nonsharp rates, and finite-time implementation.

**Dependencies and story role:** M13–M29; integrates the project without promoting one observable or limit into another theorem.

**Conceptual connection:** The same full physical projection permits position-dependent local memory while independent erasure imposes a size-independent two-way coherent-capacity ceiling.

**Sources:** [theorem](../research/THEOREM.md), [local realization](../research/LOCAL_REALIZATION.md), [capacity](../science/capacity.md), [interpretation](../science/interpretation.md).
